Full screen fills your screen with the stage (Esc to leave), with Go and Back in the corner. Editing is locked for visitors; sign in as admin to change the rig, cues and set. Rave mode moves every light over the crowd in time to a 128 BPM beat, pulsing on each beat and changing colour every bar (with Crowd sound on you hear the beat too) (the Rave cue turns it on), and Crowd sound plays a live crowd: murmuring when seated, roaring when cheering and clapping for applause. Aim and the Crowd position preset can now point lights into the audience. When you scroll away from the stage, it follows you in a small floating window with Go and Back. The Audience menu in the Set bar makes the crowd sit, cheer or applaud, and cues remember it. The Venue menu switches between the mega church (thrust stage, LED wall, side screens, worship band and choir, arena seating) and the theater. The Fog fader runs two fog machines whose fog takes the colour of the lights and makes beams show; Confetti fires the cannons, and a cue can fire them too. View: Performer puts you on stage looking out at the crowd. The audience sits in raked rows in front of the stage; the House fader sets the house lights, and each cue stores its house level (full for the Preset, out when the show starts). In the church venue the cue list runs like a Sunday service (walk-in, songs by section, prayer, message, response, benediction, walk-out); each cue has a label, time, auto-follow and an operator note, and admins can change them in Edit cue or insert point cues like 4.5. Admins can undo with Ctrl+Z (redo with Ctrl+Shift+Z or Ctrl+Y), and the admin tools above the stage publish the show to the website, set a banner, and choose what visitors can do. Your cues, rig and set save in this browser automatically; Reset show brings back the demo. The lit cross is light Cross in the fixture sheet (and the Cross group): give it any colour or level from the colour presets, encoders or command line, and each cue keeps it. New light types in the Patch list: Blinder (a warm 4-lite audience light; hold Blinder hit to flash every blinder to full), LED strobe and Laser (a fan of beams; Zoom sets the fan width and Ballyhoo spins it). The Shutter encoder page sets a strobe rate on any light; for safety the preview flashes no more than 3 times a second, and not at all if your device is set to reduce motion. Masking off hides the border and the wall above the stage so you can see the bars, and Fly bar in / out flies the electric picked in Position, lights and all. The Set list also has flying scenery: painted drops, a scrim, a border, a chandelier, a moon and a show sign. Move the view with Orbit, Pan (or right-drag / Shift-drag) and the mouse wheel; double-click resets it. Build a set with the Set bar: add scenery, furniture and actors, use the Set tool to drag them around, Rotate 45° to turn them and Fly in / out to fly scenery above the stage. Curtain opens and closes the main curtain. Each stored cue remembers where the set and actors stand, which pieces are flown, the cyc and the curtain, so the stage changes between scenes. Use Add bar and Remove bar to hang or strike electrics, and Cyc in / out to fly the cyclorama. Use Patch to add lights from the type list (moving spots, washes and beams, profiles, Fresnels and LED PARs) to any truss, change the type of selected lights, or remove them. Select lights by clicking their tags on the stage, rows in the fixture sheet, or a group. Turn the encoders (drag up and down, scroll, or use arrow keys) to set dimmer, pan, tilt, zoom and color. Use Aim and click the stage to point the selected lights at that spot. Press Store to save the look as a new cue, then Go and Back to play the cue list. The command line accepts console syntax such as Group 2 At 80, 1 Thru 3 @ Full, Store Cue 6 and Cue 3.

From first read-through to opening night

A typical mid-scale play runs ten to twelve weeks from first meeting to opening night. We join as early as the set designer's first model box.

  1. 01

    Read & concept

    Script breakdown, references and a lighting storyboard agreed with the director and set designer.

    Weeks 1–2
  2. 02

    Previs model

    We build the venue, set and masking from the CAD files, using the house's rep plot as the starting rig.

    Weeks 2–4
  3. 03

    Plot & paperwork

    The light plot and hookup go to the production electrician and rental shop for pricing.

    Weeks 4–6
  4. 04

    Offline programming

    We write the cue stack against the model and show it to the director in a previs session.

    Weeks 6–9
  5. 05

    Load-in & focus

    Hang, focus with our focus charts and check the patch against the model.

    Week 10
  6. 06

    Tech & previews

    We refine cues in the room, give notes each preview and freeze the show at opening.

    Weeks 11–12
